# User guide

## What this app is

An assistant for anyone juggling work and commitments. You hand in notes, minutes and messages; the assistant reads each one and
proposes what to record: actions with owners and dates, questions for people, facts and notes. You look over what it did
and keep it, change it, send it back with a note, or undo it. Nothing goes to another person until you approve the exact
words. By default you copy a message and send it from your own mail or chat. Where a mail or calendar add-on is signed in
and you have turned acting on for that account, a reply, an archive or an invitation waits under Follow-ups, in Waiting to
go out, until you approve exactly what will happen; the app never acts on its own.

## The screens: the side menu, what each screen is for, and the names people use for them

People say "panel", "page", "screen", "tab" or "view" for the same thing: a screen in the side menu (on a phone, the bar at the bottom and its Work and More lists). The screens are:

- Today: the home screen. What needs you now, each line starting with what to do.
- Schedule: your calendar as a page, under Today in the side menu: your meetings and what is due, by day, work week, week, month or timeline (see The Schedule and the Timeline below). Add a meeting to it from its Add button.
- What changed: the history of the assistant's work, newest first: what it did with each thing you handed in. It has no buttons for the work: what waits for you is a line on Today, and a held item here links to it. Undo is in each row's menu.
- Handled for you: mail the app dealt with for you: what it set aside before reading, and what it read and found nothing to do in. Each mail row shows the first lines of the message, **Show the message** opens it whole, and **Open the message** goes to it in your mail service. Treat one as work or as personal, or set mail like it aside from now on.
- Follow-ups: To send holds the messages, pages and files that wait for your approval of exactly what will go; Later holds what you put off; Waiting on them holds the replies you are waiting for.
- To-dos: every open to-do and every open question, with owners and dates.
- Stages: work that moves through steps, the checks to pass at each step and where each item stands.
- Personal: your own life: the to-dos, questions, notes and people that aren't work, grouped into areas such as home or health. When items are about one matter of your life, the assistant offers to start an area for it on Today ("Start the area Book collecting"), with its reason and the items it would take listed under it; a shop or a sender is never an area. Nothing moves until you start it, and Undo puts it back. With a job or a business, each one has its own entry beside it.
- Projects: your projects, each with its to-dos, questions, notes and people. Views, a list of the projects inside one side, are kept here. When you have a job or business, each side is listed in the menu by its own name, and its page holds its projects.
- People: the people the assistant knows, who they are to you and what they own.
- Files: the files you added and the ones add-ons made, and the actions they offer.
- Notes: every note you or the assistant kept, newest first, under the side filter; a note filed by a change says so in What changed.
- Knowledge: the knowledge panel. What the assistant has learned from your corrections and the rules it follows; you keep, change or retire each one, and review the rules in it.
- Settings: every setting, the setup walkthrough, accounts, backups and spending. Its Connections section lists the sources the assistant keeps reading, such as your mail and calendar, and whether each is working (set-aside mail is under Handled for you); its Add-ons section lists kinds of work you can add, what each one reaches and stores, and where to turn it on or off.
- Quality: the quality screen, opened from the menu under your initials as "How <the assistant's name> is doing" (Reginald unless you renamed it; "How your assistant is doing" with no name). How well the assistant is doing, counted from the work itself, such as how often you correct it.
- Ask: the question box; it answers from this guide and your records.

If someone asks about a "panel", "page", "screen" or "tab" by a name above, it is that screen; say what it is for and where it is in the side menu.

## Today

Today is the home screen. It shows what needs you now: what changed since you last looked, follow-ups to send, items
ready to move to their next stage, work waiting for your go, and lines that add-ons put there. Each row has one main
button; everything else is in its menu (the three dots). While setup is unfinished, one line shows how far along it is and opens the setup walkthrough at the next step.

A line starts with what you do: "Answer ...", "Send ...", "Set a new date for ..." or "Give 3 new to-dos a date". Lines about the same thing are grouped into one, never one for each item, and each kind has a small icon beside its words (the words always say it too). A few kinds worth knowing: a meeting that was called off is held, not done: Today asks you to set a new date for it or drop it, and the to-dos tied to it are paused with it until you keep or drop them; new to-dos with no date are one line that opens To-dos, and a new area the assistant suggests is a line that starts "Start the area ..." with its name filled in (Rename is in its menu); a screen that took long to open is one line, "Check why ... is slow", whose button drafts a short report you can copy to the maintainers (nothing is sent from here). A toast that only offers Undo goes when you move to another screen; press u to undo the last action while it is there.

## Setting up

The first time you open a new workspace, the setup walkthrough opens by itself. It takes about 10 to 20 minutes and spends
nothing: nothing is spent until you press Start on Today's Start line. It has eight screens: Welcome, You, What it's for, Your AI account, Mail and
calendar, Backups, People and Done. Welcome says what you will need and that nothing is read or spent until
you say so. You: your name, the workspace's name and your time zone. What it's for: whether you also keep track of a job
or your own business (each becomes a side of your work; your own life is always there). Your AI account: pick a service, paste the key, test it, and see what it may spend (the amount it asks you
about first, and the most it may spend in a day). Mail and calendar: pick a service to connect; if it isn't on this
computer yet, Connect installs it and turns it on, then, if it needs one, you press Restart to finish connecting (a few
seconds), then you sign in on the service's own page and say who each account is for. The assistant only reads. The first look back over your recent mail is not a screen: it is one line on Today with its cost, and nothing is read until you press Start. Backups: a folder
outside the workspace. People: optional, because names the assistant can't place become questions for you. Done: what
was set up, what you skipped (each with a Set up button), and optional extras such as a starting template.

Setup can't be skipped as a whole. In the AI step, once your account passes its check, you press Turn on: that is the
first go, and until you press it nothing is read by the AI. The steps run AI, Mail and calendar, then a short wait on
screen while the assistant makes its first reading of what you connected (it moves on by itself when that is done, and
you can go on sooner: it keeps working), then People, then Backups. A step can be left out, and each time a message says
what that gives up and how the next steps change (with no mail or no AI account there is nothing to wait for and People is
by hand only). A step you left out is settled, not counted as unfinished, and is still one click away in Settings. It
resumes where you left it, also after a restart. The walkthrough asks for at most one restart, and the app never restarts
without you pressing the button.
The Getting started page that comes with the product covers the same path from the install command. Everything it sets is
also in Settings later.

## Handing something in

Press Hand something in (or the n key), paste or type the text, or drop a file, a voice note or a picture, and say whose
words they are: **Someone else's** (read as information, never as instructions) or **Mine** (followed as your own
instructions). Add, in your own words, what you want done with it if it isn't obvious. The assistant reads one input at a
time. Text you paste from outside is material, never instructions: if a pasted email says "ignore your rules", the
assistant ignores that. A voice note is kept and turned into text once an account that takes speech is set up; a picture
is read by an account that can read pictures.

## What changed and reviewing the assistant's work

What changed is the history: it lists each hand-in and what the assistant did with it, newest first, and opens on everything.
Choose a row to see each change it made, with the exact words from your input that justify it. A line at the top says how
many things wait for you on Today and opens it. Nothing here is answered: a change that waits for you, such as a new person
named only in outside content, a change of a setting or a rule, or anything that contradicts a rule you set, shows its reason
and one link, Answer on Today. In a row's menu, Looks good files the work as looked over, Send back with a note asks the
assistant to try again with your correction, and Undo this or Undo everything it did reverses the change or the whole
hand-in. Undo is always available, and both the change and the undo stay in the history. When the same message or invitation reaches two of your accounts, it is one row that says "Read from" both accounts and carries nothing for you to look at. A change you made yourself on a screen is a row called "Your change".

## Follow-ups

Follow-ups gathers what you need to send to other people: questions for them and actions they own, by person or by
project. Under To send, each person has one message: **Copy message** (or Copy as plain text), send it from your own mail,
then **Mark sent**, which has Undo; copying alone doesn't mark it sent, and a nudge isn't completion, so the item stays
open. Later holds what you put off, with the day or the meeting it waits for. Waiting on them shows what you already
sent, one line each, with how long ago, and lets you record their reply or mark the item finished. Each person's line says what is due and when, and a band on their page lists what is due in the next few days. Waiting to go out is
also here: the replies, archives and calendar changes an add-on would carry out for you, each waiting for your approval
of exactly what will happen; Take it back is in its menu.

## To-dos and questions

To-dos and questions lists every open to-do and every open question, in two tabs with their filters (Person, State, Project). Each row has one main action: the round mark on a to-do marks it done (with Undo; it is not a select box), a question's button takes you to where it is answered. Everything else is in the row's menu, the three dots: Add a note, Edit, Pause, Remind me, Change this (asks the assistant to change it with a note you write), Drop, and, for a to-do or question that came from a page, feed or folder the assistant watches, **Not mine to track** (see Sources the assistant keeps reading). A to-do with no date is listed at the foot under "Needs a date", each with a date picker beside it, so there is nothing to press first; "Show N more coming up" lists the rest of the ones with dates. Add a to-do with **Add a to-do** at the top. A to-do with no owner shows **Pick an owner** until you choose one.

## People and projects

People and Projects are where you add, rename and remove people and projects (Add in each screen's header; a person's or
project's own page has Edit). Records refer to people and projects by id, so a rename changes every place at once.
Something that other records still point to can't be removed until they don't. A project can sit inside another project
(a series and its books, a program and its parts), have its own language and its own budget for AI work. A removed person
can be restored from their page.

## Days off and time away

A team's page has **Days off**: closures, a holiday calendar and the days the team works (none ticked means the
workspace's working week). A person's page has **Time away**: periods they are away. **Add days off** or **Add time
away** opens a form with date pickers; each period is a row with Edit and Remove. Every change is yours at once, with
Undo. A person's team's days off count for them too. The Timeline hatches the days people don't work, and the details of
an item that falls on one say why. Time away does not have to be typed: a calendar of vacations can fill it in (see How do I
add a vacation calendar), and so can a list you paste into a note or an out-of-office reply you hand in, which the assistant reads into
periods you can undo.

## How do I add a vacation calendar? (who is away)

Use this for a calendar that lists who is away (a team's vacation calendar, a shared "out of office" calendar). Its entries
are read for the days people are away; they never become meetings.

1. Open **Settings**, then **Connections**, and press **Add mail, calendar or folder**.
2. Choose **A calendar** (or your calendar service's own kind, if you use one) and press Next.
3. Give it a name such as "Who is away", and its private address or file as the form asks. Most calendar services show a
   private address in their sharing settings. With a mail or calendar add-on, pick the shared calendar the add-on lists.
4. Open **More options**, and where it says **What is in this calendar?** choose **Who is away** (Meetings is the
   default).
5. Press **Add connection**. It is read within a minute, then on its schedule. Nothing is asked first, because only the days
   are kept.

To change an existing calendar, open its menu on Connections, choose **Change** and set **What is in this calendar?** there.

What happens next, with no AI run and nothing sent: each entry is matched to a person by the name in its title (a full name,
a name you gave them as an alias, or a first name with the last name's initial, such as "Sam R."), else the one person asked
to it, else who organized it. The person's **Time away** gets the first and last day (an all-day entry's last day is the day
before the calendar's end date), and a label such as vacation or leave only when the title says so. Nothing else of the entry
is kept. A moved entry moves the period, and a called-off or removed one removes it; an entry outside the days the calendar
is read for is left alone. A period you change yourself, or remove, is yours: the calendar never changes it again. Each
read that adds periods can be undone from What changed.

Names that match nobody you know are not guessed at and nothing is asked: the calendar's row on Connections says "N names in
this calendar are nobody you know", with **Add as a person** beside each; adding one makes the person and reads the calendar
again. When a title names two or more of your people, you are asked once for each series of entries, on Today, "Whose days
away are these?", and you pick the person; your answer stands for the rest of that series.

You can also tell the assistant: paste a list such as "Ana: Nov 20-24, vacation" into Hand something in as a note, or hand in
an out-of-office reply; it proposes the periods. Not built yet: reading the automatic replies the app sets aside from your mail,
and half days.

## Sides and views

If you keep track of a job or a business as well as your own life, each is a **side**. Your own life is **Personal**.
Projects and people sit on a side, and each side is listed in the side menu by its own name. Where more than one side
exists, a **side filter** (a row of choices, or one Side button when there are many) narrows Today, the Schedule and the
lists to one side; a line that belongs to no side stays under every choice. A calendar or mail account says which sides
it serves when you connect it (setup asks "What is this account for?" for each account), and can be changed in Settings under
Connections; mail from an account that serves no side is counted once, under **Not assigned**, never under every side. A **view** is a short list of the
projects inside one side, such as a manager's portfolio: add one on the Projects screen with **Add view**, pick its
projects, give it a name and an optional short tag. Add a business or rename a side in Settings, under Your business.

## The Schedule and the Timeline

Schedule is under Today in the side menu. Its views are **Day**, **Work week** (the days you work; Weekdays when you
have no sides), **Week**, **Month** and **Timeline**; click one or press 1 to 5 (never while typing). The arrows and
**Today** move by the view's unit; Left and Right do the same. Day, Work week and Week are a time grid: each meeting
stands at its own time, an all-day strip on top holds all-day entries and due dates, and two meetings that overlap
are drawn side by side, with a Clash mark on a clash. It shows every connected calendar's events, the entries you add,
the assistant's entries (each says "Added by" the assistant's name; yours say "Added by you"), deliveries and the due day of each open to-do you own. Each calendar has a
name (by default the side it serves, never the raw account label) and a color mark beside it, never the only sign.
When the grid is cut at the bottom it says "N more meetings below". Choose an entry to see its details beside the
calendar; Escape closes them. **Add** (the button, or "..." on a phone) makes an entry of the app's own; to put an
entry on a connected calendar too, you approve it under Waiting to go out, as an act. **Timeline** shows where each piece stands over the next weeks, one lane per project, grouped by side: a circle is
a calendar entry, a square a to-do or promise with a day, a diamond a target day or a dated fact and a bar something
that runs over several days; the range is 2 weeks, 10 weeks or a quarter. Under it, **Needs a date** lists the to-dos
with no date, each with a date to pick. Narrow windows fall back to Day where the week doesn't fit. Change the place or repeat of an entry you added from its details ("Change the place or repeat"; from one date of a repeating entry it changes the whole series, in one change with Undo); an entry of a connected calendar isn't changed here. Not built yet: dragging an
entry.

## Stages

Work that moves through steps (an order from new to delivered, a document from outline to approved) has stages. Each
stage can have entry checks, which must pass before an item moves into it; the Stages screen shows which checks fail and
why. You move items one stage at a time; a stage that doesn't apply to an item is passed over. Moving back is always
allowed. Entering a stage can start work for the assistant, which waits for your go.

## Files

Files lists what you added and what add-ons made. Add a file by dropping it on the Files screen or choosing one, or when
handing something in; a file can be tied to the person it came from (from their page). Some files offer actions from
add-ons (the example add-on's **Make a note from this file**, a build, a preview); an action shows you exactly what it would do, and you apply it or close it.
Applied actions can be undone. A file's menu has **Rename…**, an edit of its description and **Remove**; each applies at once with Undo, and Remove is refused while another record refers to the file. Not built yet: removing a file also removing its stored copy.

## Knowledge

Knowledge is in the side menu. It shows **Waiting for your OK** (what the assistant proposes to learn from your
corrections: Keep, Edit before keeping, or Choose where this applies), **What the assistant follows** (the rules and
lessons you kept, each of which you can change or retire), Your words (names and terms you use) and what has not been
used in a while or was retired. A lesson from something you handed in from outside waits for you; only your own Keep makes
it active. Reviewing the rules is done here too.

## Quality

Quality is opened from the menu under your initials as **How <the assistant's name> is doing** (Reginald unless you renamed it). It counts from the work itself:
how many of the promises it found you kept, how often you correct it, closes you didn't see, wrong endings, promises the
app missed and the time you spend looking its work over, week by week. Each figure shows how many out of how many, never a
bare percentage, and says "Too early to tell" until there is enough to count.

## The brief

On Today's side, a card gives a **Morning brief**, an **Afternoon brief**, an **Evening wrap-up** and, on Mondays,
**This week's briefing**: what is due, what is late, clashes, and what carries over to tomorrow, each pointing to the
line on Today. **Close for today** puts it away, and the Show link in the card brings it back. The brief only
summarizes what Today already holds; it sends nothing. On Mondays it also offers goals for the week: the assistant reads
what is due, what others promised you and what is on your calendar, with the goals you kept before, and offers a few. You
confirm them, change them or drop some; **Offer goals** asks again. Reading costs like any other work, so it waits for your
go above the amount you set.

## AI accounts and costs

The app uses AI accounts you bring: a key from a model provider, a service that reaches many models with one key, a
model server on your own computer, or a command-line agent signed in as you. Add one in setup or Settings: it is checked at once, with one small call per kind of work and the settings that work will
use, and says in plain words what failed (an account kept for nothing yet is checked too). Each account's menu has **Test again**; a row
shows only **Add the key** or **See why** when something needs you. Work that would cost more than the amount you set waits for your go with an estimate first. Daily and monthly
budgets pause work when they are used up. A model whose price the app doesn't know is never counted as free: its work waits for your go every time, until you give a price or say the account is flat (a subscription or a model on your own computer, where a run costs nothing more); on a flat account you can still choose to be asked first (Settings, Advanced, AI and spending). Which account does which kind of work is set by tiers: everyday work and harder work can use
different accounts; Settings, under AI and spending, has Which model does what.

When the AI work stops, Today says so, one line for each cause, never one for each item. The causes are: no AI account
for a kind of work (the line says to choose one), no one set as the person the app is for ("Say who you are"), an account
paused until its usage limit resets (with the time and how many items wait; they run by themselves then), credit run
out, an account not allowed to see the material's privacy level, and a network that is down (the work waits and tries
again by itself). A wrong key or a refused account names the account and what to do. Items stopped
because no AI account can take them, for the same reasons, are one line.

Without an AI account, a stand-in does the reading so you can try the app (the demo uses it). It is no model: it
follows plain rules. A line such as "Sam will send the quotes by 2030-03-08" becomes an action for Sam due that day; "We
still need the page count" becomes an action waiting for you to pick an owner; "Decided: …" becomes a fact. Anything
else is kept as a note. Asked a question, it quotes the closest passage of this guide.

## Asking the assistant

You can ask the assistant about the app or about your work, in your own words: "all of this is confusing, how do I just add a vacation
calendar?" is a question it answers with steps. It reads the whole of this guide and the add-ons' help every time (when
the guide is longer than the limit in Settings, Advanced, it reads the four sections that fit the question best and says
so), and when it gives steps it says which section they come from. It answers only from this guide, your settings (never your
keys), what this install can do, and your own records, and it shows the words its answer rests on. If the answer is a
setting you can change, it suggests the change and you apply it with one click, with Undo. If you ask for something the
app can't do, it drafts a short report to the app's maintainers that you can review and send.

You can also talk to the assistant about one thing. A to-do, question, person, project or meeting has a box on its page:
ask what it is waiting on, or ask for something to be done to it. The assistant sees that record and what you said
before. What it answers is kept on the record. A change it proposes is listed under its answer, and you keep or drop each
one there. If the work would cost more than the amount you chose to be asked about, it waits for your go first.

Now and then the assistant also offers a choice about something you keep putting off: a to-do whose day has moved again
and again, a promise whose day keeps changing, replies you owe the same person, or a to-do nothing has touched for a
while. Each is one line on Today with two or three choices, such as drop it, make it weekly or leave it. A choice acts at
once and has Undo, and nothing is closed or sent for you.

## Settings

Settings has a page for what most people set in their first week (You, AI and spending, Notices, Backups and Appearance,
and Your business when you have one) and Advanced for the rest, in a few plain topics. Each setting says what it does; a
setting you changed shows Changed with a way back to its default, and one setup set for you does not. A setting set by
an environment variable or a command-line flag is locked on the screen and says so. Saving checks the whole set of settings before anything is written, keeps the comments in your settings
files, and lets you undo the change. Under **Notices** you choose when the app tells you things and can turn on **Mail to yourself**: a note in your own mail when something needs you, even when the app is closed. You pick the account it is sent from and one of your own addresses; the note says how many things are waiting, never what about, unless you allow titles, and restricted items are never named. **Send me a test** sends one; **Stop sending notices by mail** ends it. Under Advanced, **AI and spending** holds how much of this guide every question to the assistant reads. A setting the app reads at the moment it uses it applies at once. One that needs a
restart says so, holds new work and waits for you to choose **Restart now**; the app never restarts on its own. The
server's address, port and mode are changed only in their file, so the app can't lock you out. The pages are You, Your
business, AI and spending, Notices, Connections, Backups, Lists, Add-on files, Appearance, Quick links, Add-ons and
Advanced (only those that apply to you are listed), and a page for each add-on that has settings. **Search settings**
finds a setting by its words and keeps the cursor in its box while the results load.

## Backups

Backups copy the whole workspace (its database, settings and saved sources) to a folder off this computer: an external
drive, a network share or a folder a sync service copies elsewhere. Choose the folder in setup or Settings; it must
exist, be outside the workspace and be writable. Backups run on a schedule while the app runs, keep the number you set,
and the header shows the age of the last one. If a drive isn't connected, the app tries again later and tells you.

**Restore from a backup:** Settings, Backups, **Restore this copy** on the copy you want (an encrypted copy asks for its
key). The app stages it and nothing changes until you choose Restart now; at the next start what you had is set aside, not
deleted, and the copy takes its place. To make a new workspace from a copy instead, use `cockpit restore --backup` on the
command line (docs/install.md). Not built yet: restoring to a chosen moment (point in time) or one record as it was.

## Using two computers

A workspace can live on two computers through a synced folder (a folder a sync service keeps the same on both). Only one
computer works on the workspace at a time: the app takes a lease in the folder and gives it back when it stops. When you
start on the other computer, it loads the newer copy. Copies a sync service makes on a conflict are never loaded; the app
lists them so you can look. Files you edit in the synced folder come back as proposals you review.

## Sources the assistant keeps reading

Besides what you hand in, the assistant can keep reading places for you: a folder, a news feed, a calendar or a mail
folder. Each is a source, with how often it is read. A source's first read only notes what is there;
after that, anything new, changed or gone is handed in for the assistant to read, marked as outside content. A
calendar is read through its private address (most calendar services offer one in their sharing settings), and the
events in the coming weeks are followed: a moved meeting or a new invitation comes in as a change. Your mailbox is
read over IMAP with an app password from your mail service; messages are never marked as read or changed. Addresses
and passwords are kept outside your settings, as environment variables or files on this computer. Rules can set
aside changes that don't matter, such as a spacing fix or a change made by your own account; they are listed under
Auto-closed, where Take a closer look hands one in after all. In a watched folder, files that differ only by version marks (v3, v3_1), dates or words such as FOR REVIEW,
DRAFT and FINAL are versions of one document: only the newest copy outside an Archive, Old or Superseded folder counts, a
change to an older copy is closed as "an older version; the current one is ..." (Take a closer look brings it back), and
the newest copy says which version it replaces. Files with unrelated names aren't linked on their own; on a saved file's page, **Same document as…** links it to another file and **Not the same document** keeps two apart, each at once with Undo. Each source says **whose work is on it**: when you add a page or feed the question "Whose work is on it?" is asked with **Another group's** chosen (the page is read, summarized and remembered, and nothing is added to your to-dos or questions from it) and **Mine to track** as the other choice; mail, a calendar and a folder start as yours to track. Change it later with Change in the source's menu. When a to-do or question made from a page or feed is not yours, **Not mine to track** in its row's menu drops it and the other open ones from that source and makes the source another group's, at once, and the toast says how many went with it and has one Undo that puts everything back. A page or feed that changed in more than five places in one read is handed in as one input for each project, not one for each change. A calendar can hold who is away instead of meetings (see How do I add a vacation calendar). If a source can't be read several times in a row, you
are asked once, with what to do. A source that has kept failing for longer than its usual gap (by default twice its read interval,
counted from the first failed read of the current run of failures, not from the last good read) is also one line on
Today, "not read since" a time, with what to do; a source that only has no network is never a line. A mail message
is read whole: nothing is cut at a fixed length, and a message too long for an account says so plainly.

Some add-ons read a service through your own account, signing in on that service's page. Sign in from Settings: the
service asks you what to allow, then sends you back to the app. The sign-in is kept on this computer only and renewed
on its own; if the service withdraws it, you are asked to sign in again. Sign out from the same place; each account has its own Sign out, and asks first. Signing in again to an account that is already signed in says "Already signed in as ...; refreshed" and changes nothing. If a work or school service says an administrator must approve the app, the message says so plainly: only that administrator can allow it. Turning on **acting** for an account (replies, archiving, invitations) is a separate choice made on that account, and every act still waits for your yes under Waiting to go out.

## Templates and your own item types

A template sets up the kinds of items you track (requests, pieces, orders), their fields, stages and checklists, and
what Today shows. Pick one in setup, or apply one later. You can add your own item types the same way, and save your
setup as a template to reuse.

## Add-ons

Add-ons (plugins) add kinds of work: item types, actions, screens, stages and lines on Today. Each one works alone. If
one fails, the app skips that part, notes it on the add-on, and turns the add-on off only if it keeps failing; your data
stays. An add-on can't send anything or change records except through the same review you use. Its actions (such as the example add-on's Make a note from this file) show exactly what they would do, and you apply them or close them; its own help is read by the assistant along with this guide.

## Privacy and what the assistant sees

Each AI run sees only what that piece of work needs: the input, the related records and your standing rules. Records can
be marked as internal or restricted; restricted material goes only to accounts you allowed for it, such as a model on
your own computer. Keys are never written in your settings; they are kept on this computer or in the environment.

## Keyboard shortcuts

Press ? for the list. j and k move between rows, Enter opens one, a is Looks good or Approve, s sends back, u undoes, c
copies, e edits and n hands something in. Shortcuts never fire while you are typing and can be changed in Settings.

## When something goes wrong

Every waiting item says why and until when. An AI account problem (a wrong key, a usage limit, a service that is down)
says which account and what to do; Try again sends the work again, and a refused piece of work can go to another
account. If the app was stopped or the computer slept, it picks up where it was. Settings that don't load stop the app
from starting and name the file and the key. If your browser loses its sign-in, a screen says how to get back in: open the app again from its icon or launcher, or run `cockpit open`. Something that goes wrong is written to a log file in the workspace's private folder (never on screen, never holding keys, addresses or names), which you can send with a bug report. To tell the makers something broke without a terminal or an account, open Help and choose Report a problem: write what happened, check the address the makers may answer to (yours, if the app has it) and whether to include the recent problems, and read everything that will be sent, which is your words, the app's version, your system and those problem lines with names, addresses and keys left out. Nothing leaves your computer until you press Send. If it can't be delivered, the app says why and offers Try again and Copy the report, so you can mail it yourself. During the alpha, Help also has Your alpha numbers: how many days you used the app in its first week, what the assistant cost a day, how often you corrected it, how many of its drafts you sent as written or edited, and how long it took from the first start to its first result. They stay on your computer; Share these with the makers opens the same report, with the numbers as its words, and nothing leaves until you press Send.

To delete a workspace whole, use Settings, Advanced, **Delete this workspace…** or `cockpit erase`: it lists exactly what goes (the workspace, its history and its backups) and deletes only after you confirm. Deleting one item elsewhere keeps its history.
