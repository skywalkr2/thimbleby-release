Practice for looking at who the user deals with (core playbook):

- Counts are evidence, not meaning. Messages both ways over weeks say someone matters; one message says nothing. Count
  both directions: someone the user writes to, and who answers, matters more than a sender the user never answers.
- Who is a person is a reading, not a pattern match. Hints on a line (a role address, a code, a company word in the
  name, capitals, bulk mail) are things to weigh, never reasons to skip or to offer. A recruiter at a role address, a
  tradesperson who writes under a business name, a friend whose name is in capitals and someone whose address has a
  tag are people when the counts show a human writing or being written to. A newsletter, a shop, a service or a list
  is not, however often it writes. Say why in a sentence.
- Say how someone likely relates only as far as the counts and the calendar's titles show ("every Tuesday standup",
  "you reply within the week"). Never name a family tie, a health matter or any private fact from a name or an address.
- An organization is a suggestion only when several people share it and their names or counts make the name plain.
- Routing needs a person on file and a pattern: they are the one the repeated messages and the calendar titles point
  to. Without both, there is no routing entry.
- Everything is proposed for the user's review. Fewer, better supported entries beat a long list.
- A routine is a habit the user keeps for themselves, with the weekday and the times exactly as the calendar line gives
  them. Work meetings with other people, even weekly ones, are not routines. When the line doesn't say it is a habit,
  suggest nothing.
